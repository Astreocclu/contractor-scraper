## 5. Gaps/Concerns

### Critical Gaps

1. **Google Local Services Omission**
   - Google LSA verifies licenses, does background checks, offers money-back guarantee
   - "Google Guaranteed" badge is a major trust signal for homeowners
   - This directly undercuts the claim that "no one does verification well"
   - **Impact:** Positioning strategy needs revision

2. **Buildzoom Not Mentioned**
   - Buildzoom DOES use permit data for contractor evaluation
   - They have "Remodel Score" based on permit history
   - This directly competes with Greenlit's permit-verification angle
   - **Impact:** Claims of uniqueness need qualification

3. **Greenlit Capabilities Assumed, Not Verified**
   - Document assumes ELO scoring, continuous monitoring, permit verification are operational
   - No evidence these are deployed or tested
   - **Impact:** Sales team could oversell capabilities

### Moderate Concerns

4. **Dated Snapshots Risk**
   - Competitor ratings (BBB scores, Sitejabber reviews) change over time
   - Platform pricing and features evolve
   - Document will age quickly without update protocol

5. **Homeowner Behavior Gap**
   - No actual data on how DFW homeowners currently find contractors
   - Assumes platform usage patterns without local validation
   - Survey or interview data would strengthen this

6. **No Competitive Response Analysis**
   - What happens when Angi notices Greenlit?
   - Do competitors have permit-data capabilities they're not using?
   - What barriers prevent competitors from copying Greenlit's approach?

### Minor Concerns

7. **Sample Size on BBB/Sitejabber Scores**
   - Citing review scores from complaint sites (17 reviews, 60 reviews) as if meaningful
   - Small sample sizes on complaint sites don't prove much
   - More rigorous: look at total review volume relative to platform size

## 6. Greenlit Implications

Based on this analysis, the three most important things Greenlit should emphasize in contractor outreach:

### #1: "We Verify What Others Self-Report" (85/100 Confidence)

**The Angle:** Every major platform admits contractors "self-report" credentials. Greenlit pulls permit data directly from municipal databases.

**Why It Works:**
- FOX 4 investigation proves local verification failures
- Contractors who ARE properly licensed resent competing with unlicensed operators
- This is defensible (permits are public record) and differentiating

**Caveat:** Verify Greenlit actually has permit data for the specific cities being sold into.

### #2: "Quality Determines Rank, Not Ad Spend" (75/100 Confidence)

**The Angle:** Pay-to-play perception is strong across all platforms. Contractors feel trapped in advertising wars.

**Why It Works:**
- Documented contractor frustration with lead costs eating 70-80% of profit
- Small/medium contractors feel buried by larger competitors' ad budgets
- Appeals to "underdog" positioning that quality contractors identify with

**Caveat:** Only works if Greenlit ACTUALLY has a quality-based ranking system that isn't just a different pay-to-play model.

### #3: "Stop Paying for Shared Leads" (70/100 Confidence)

**The Angle:** 3-15 contractors per lead is documented across major platforms. Race-to-respond wastes contractor time.

**Why It Works:**
- Direct financial pain point contractors experience
- Exclusive leads would be genuinely different
- Clear value proposition: "$120 for shared lead vs. our exclusive lead model"

**Caveat:** Need to define what "exclusive" actually means in Greenlit's model. If homeowner still gets 3 choices, is it really exclusive?

### What NOT to Emphasize

- **"Only platform with trust scores"** - Google LSA exists. Claim is false.
- **"ELO-based scoring"** - Too technical for sales. Contractors don't know/care what ELO means.
- **"Objective data"** - Abstract. Lead with permit verification as the mechanism.
